## Overview

Sande Technologies is a brand site for a remote engineering partner. It should feel like a capable distributed product team: direct, calm, visibly organised, and grounded in tangible delivery rather than abstract “innovation” language. The signature is the hero delivery board—an editorial interpretation of active work that proves the company’s remote-team positioning at first glance.

## Colors

Ink and navy establish operational confidence. Cobalt is reserved for committed actions and key links; signal cyan is an evidence/highlight color, never a full-page wash. Cloud creates breathing room between concentrated dark sections. Use semantic red only for form errors.

## Typography

Manrope is the compact, generous display face. Inter carries service descriptions and form copy. Headlines use tight tracking and strong line breaks; body copy is measured and never oversized. Avoid all-caps promotional labels.

## Layout

Content lives in a 1200px frame with generous vertical pacing. The hero uses an asymmetric two-column composition: positioning and decisions on the left, delivery evidence on the right. Service cards use a numbered left rail because the offer is a genuinely navigable six-part practice.

## Elevation & Depth

Surfaces are primarily outlined rather than floated. Only the hero board uses a deeper shadow to create one focal object. Use very subtle borders over layered shadows.

## Shapes

Corners are softened but not pill-like. Buttons are compact rounded rectangles; tags are small status capsules.

## Components

Navigation is quiet with a clear active/hover treatment. Primary buttons are cobalt; secondary buttons are transparent with an ink border. Interactive elements show a cobalt focus ring. Forms keep labels visible above fields, preserve their geometry on validation, and announce submission status in place.

## Do's and Don'ts

Do write like a practical technology partner and show how an engagement works. Do use imagery sparingly and give it a clear role. Don’t rely on generic gradients, stock-office imagery, inflated claims, or a grid of identical floating cards. Respect reduced motion and maintain contrast on dark surfaces.
